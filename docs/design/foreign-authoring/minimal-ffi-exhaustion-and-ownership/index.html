Exhaustion-safe FFI reporting and ownership preservation

Development design notes · ash-lang 67c79dd

Informative design work. Proposals and experiments may be incomplete or superseded. Read the language specification for current rules.

All design notes

This design refinement belongs to DN-0057 and the copied-text and connection/statement cases. It resolves their remaining reporting/ownership policy at design level, subject to review, semantic adoption and implementation evidence. It selects no source syntax and authorizes no implementation.

Common contract and existing limits

These rules are platform-independent. They require an executor to support admission, bounded control records, unique owner tracking and disposition reporting. Isolated execution additionally requires session invalidation, termination requests and observation of actual process termination. Exact platform mechanisms belong to a deployment profile; this document requires no particular executable format, syscall, allocator or process-control API.

Current Ash's native-buffer execution prepares storage and release work before native allocation, keeps internal exhaustion terminal, and gives no unconditional teardown guarantee. Its typed failure reporting keeps body outcomes separate from completion errors. These are constraints to preserve, not evidence that general FFI already exists. The experimental runtime and evaluator additionally distinguish consumed and retained owners from fallible error conversion. Transfer that distinction without adopting the whole experimental adapter architecture.

Failure classes

The canonical precedence and domain table own classification. Evaluator/parent mandatory capacity failure is terminal to that evaluation. Isolated worker-runtime exhaustion is session failure to a healthy parent; a worker report cannot establish parent exhaustion. Returned native NOMEM remains a native outcome, not evidence about either runtime.

A declared limit is an ordinary outcome only when its small result envelope has already been admitted. Optional diagnostics have a separate bounded allowance and may be skipped before allocation. Actual evaluator allocation failure stays terminal. No global budget refill or retry follows refusal.

Pre-admission and reserved records

Before native acquisition or an effectful call, reserve the control storage and fixed host work needed for all of that call's admitted outcomes:

  • Exact contract/operation, actual owner references, call phase and native status slots; no formatted strings or dynamically copied provenance.
  • Acquisition guard, fresh activation and dependency edges before acquiring a connection or statement, including partial-acquisition outcomes.
  • Scope/access publication storage and its bounded success/failure envelope.
  • Separate local-completion K and native-disposition records, ordered report slots, quarantine capacity, and dependency-ordered host-teardown bookkeeping for each admitted owner.
  • For isolation, pending-request/session record, bounded outcome-frame storage, validation work, invalidation flag and supervisor shutdown/reaping records.

Reservations precede native entry/dispatch, after operand evaluation as needed. Earlier operand effects are not rolled back on refusal. Reservations cannot be consumed by ordinary computation or optional diagnostic construction. Bound identity counters and refuse before entry rather than wrap or reuse IDs.

Payload sizes not yet known, such as a column length, require a second check before allocating/copying that payload. The already reserved control record can retain the outcome of failing that check without allocating again. Pre-admitting a control record does not reserve an arbitrary result graph.

Each owner reserves its own eventual disposition slot, not an unbounded event history. Retain ordered completion records until the enclosing report has been delivered or the runtime failure path has retained them. Charge retained history against explicit capacity; sequential acquisitions do not bypass that limit by releasing live-owner slots. Refuse new acquisition before native entry when the required report capacity is unavailable. Emergency records must not silently discard ownership facts or claim a truncated list complete.

Fixed control/report capacity is a bounded host guarantee under an intact runtime. It does not reserve SQLite's internal allocations, make arbitrary source cleanup executable after exhaustion, or ensure native termination.

Ownership changes precede reporting

Track whether the call was not entered, entered but unresolved, or returned with a recorded outcome. Store native status, protocol transition and known consumption/retention in reserved slots immediately after a conforming return, before diagnostic queries, encoding, allocation or source-result construction. This host bookkeeping step must not allocate or charge unreserved work.

Only the unique owner record establishes consumption. Access aliases, ordinary error constructors and optional diagnostics cannot change it. Once consumed, clear the native access and remove its dependency edge exactly once; later failure must not resurrect the owner or cause another native finish. Retained or uncertain ownership cannot authorize source reuse or backing release.

Acquisition installs an unpublished guard immediately when the native contract establishes an owner. Publish access only after its dependency/activation and source-result admission succeed. If publication fails, the guard survives and follows its admitted disposal/quarantine path. A contradictory native address is not a conformingly acquired owner; follow the ABI profile's quarantine rule instead of calling a destructor through suspect output.

Minimal reporting and source outcomes

Keep a fixed runtime failure record separate from ordinary Ash values. It contains cause/phase, operation and owner references, known native status, disposition, diagnostic-availability reason and report-completeness state. Use bounded IDs and references into already retained metadata. Creating this record requires no heap allocation, native diagnostic query or source callback. Rendering, localization and external output are later fallible operations; failure to render cannot change the retained ownership facts.

For normal source completion, preserve the body result separately from ordered completion reports. K1–K9 in the canonical table govern exact-once local discharge. Session loss or quarantine permits local closure with a failure record, never a stale native finish. A consumed or retained finish outcome is recorded and discharges K before optional diagnostics; report failure cannot recreate either authority. Reserve the envelope and report links before the relevant ownership transitions. Materializing richer ordinary data may still fail; that is terminal runtime failure, not a fabricated successful source return. The runtime record can retain a reference to an already obtained body result without promising to serialize or deliver it after exhaustion.

Primary runtime failure is not replaced by a later diagnostic or teardown failure. Preserve separately the native status already observed, each known owner disposition and subsequent completion/teardown failures. Missing detail is marked unavailable, never translated to successful release. This record is runtime evidence, not a first-class proof or source recovery mechanism.

Copied text in-process

Admit fixed control/error delivery before entering the copy sequence. After kind, pointer and matching length checks, reserve copy/UTF-8 work and payload storage before reading the native interval. A configured length refusal is an ordinary boundary outcome; exhausted runtime work/storage is terminal.

If admission fails, publish no string and close temporary access. The statement remains in its known row state if native behavior conformed; terminal failure does not continue the source body merely because that state is known. If native text conversion reports NOMEM, retain that native status distinctly from a later Ash allocation failure. Preserve the ABI profile's immediate error-probe ordering; do not attempt optional diagnostics first.

Copy into unpublished storage and expose only the bytes actually written. UTF-8 rejection publishes no string. Successful publication transfers the independent value; later reporting or resource failure cannot invalidate it. An incomplete copy never becomes an empty successful string. Release scratch without granting an escaping native view or retrying the operation.

Connection and statement in-process

Use L1–L22 of the canonical lifecycle table for partial acquisition, unpublished-owner disposal and consumption/retention. O4 records disposition before fallible reporting; O8 preserves it on evaluator exhaustion. No second error-to-ownership mapping is defined here.

Consumed statement access cannot reappear even when source error construction fails. Known retained dependencies block enclosing destruction before native entry. Partial-owner disposal may itself fail or fail to return; neither reserved bookkeeping nor a destructor invocation proves release succeeded.

Isolated control and payload delivery

Reserve parent pending-operation and acquisition/proxy capacity before sending any request bytes. The worker must reserve its guard/control capacity before native entry, but the parent never trusts it to have done so. Give every operation a fixed-size, independently bounded outcome frame, followed only by its declared bounded value payload and optional diagnostic payload. The outcome frame contains expected session/request/operation/resource identities, native outcome/status and permitted ownership/protocol disposition. Frames have no arbitrary embedded allocation size, pointer or source authority.

Validate the complete outcome frame and expected transition with pre-admitted parent work/storage. A partial header, unvalidated frame or out-of-order reply establishes no completion. Once a complete frame is admitted, commit the known disposition before allocating or decoding later payloads. It is still a foreign report under the stated assumptions, not proof of worker behavior.

Apply O1–O10 and X1–X5 from the canonical tables: preserve admitted control before later payload failure; distinguish worker exhaustion from parent exhaustion; never infer successful source delivery from control alone. Optional diagnostic draining is bounded and pre-admitted, or the session ends.

An admitted native outcome without a required payload is not successful source delivery. Parent runtime exhaustion terminates evaluation; its already admitted supervisor path can invalidate/stop the worker without allocating a fresh request or relying on its cooperation. Oversized/malformed frames lose the session rather than cause unbounded buffering or draining. A worker that cannot send its reserved outcome may die; the parent still follows the unknown-completion path. No acknowledgement is presumed to arrive under OOM.

Terminal disposal and executor obligations

On evaluator exhaustion X1, stop ordinary source evaluation and transfer unfinished K through K8 without claiming source cleanup. Do not invoke arbitrary source cleanup, replay completion, increase limits or promise source recovery. Worker exhaustion X4 instead loses that session: a healthy parent can handle the boundary failure and discharge its local scopes through K4 in order. Transfer live guards to already reserved host disposal records on terminal source evaluation, separately from local discharge in a healthy parent. Use retained dependency order; never discard backing while a dependent may still need it. Record whether native completion was unattempted, returned or unresolved so host teardown cannot replay an operation already known consumed.

In-process teardown may invoke only the separately admitted native disposal path, not source code. It needs an explicit contract for each still-live owner. If a finish already refused, do not silently retry it as guard destruction. Retain/quarantine the owner and required backing when safe release cannot be established. This may retain native storage until host termination; capacity remains charged. There is no eventual-release or native-call time bound in this mode. External termination can prevent all subsequent reporting.

In isolation, the supervisor requests termination using reserved platform control resources, observes actual termination and reaps before retiring the session's reservations. Until then, keep the session inaccessible, its grant reserved and its termination state explicit. Native completion not already acknowledged stays unknown; process reclamation is a different fact. Preserve persistent data/journals. Platform mechanisms and their limits belong to the launch profile, not this common contract.

Closure evidence

Fault-inject each fallible reservation before entry and each conversion after return, separately from native NOMEM. Cover exact budget, one-short budget and a fresh sufficient run without retrying effects from the failed execution. Verify no partial value/owner publication and no loss of primary failure.

For copied text, distinguish native conversion failure, configured size refusal, runtime allocation refusal, invalid UTF-8 and isolated payload loss. For ownership, inject failure after open returns a partial owner, after prepare returns a statement, after finalize consumption and after close retention. Observe exact-once consumption, preserved dependencies, unavailable diagnostics and no source continuation after terminal exhaustion.

For isolation, fail before dispatch, during a partial outcome frame, after a complete consumed frame and during subsequent payload/diagnostic delivery. Exhaust normal evaluation while a request is pending and verify the supervisor does not need new allocation to invalidate and request termination. Test delayed reaping and refusal of further admission while reservations remain charged. Reserve-capacity accounting must include retained report history and quarantine.

Together with the canonical transitions, SQLite policy, checked representation and explicit target/executor profiles, this supplies proposed design rules for the four identified blockers. The canonical tables own their combined meaning. It is not implementation or production-readiness evidence. Semantic adoption, architecture decisions and authorized delivery with positive/negative checks remain necessary. Syntax, exact runtime structs, wire encoding and platform API implementations remain unselected.
