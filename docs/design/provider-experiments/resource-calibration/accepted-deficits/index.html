Adopted calibration with accepted deficits, 2026-10-08

Development design notes · ash-lang 67c79dd

Informative design work. Proposals and experiments may be incomplete or superseded. Read the language specification for current rules.

All design notes

This is the offline adoption of the user's calibrated decision-support measurements under TASK-0035 and DN-0036. Common coordinates the separate compiler admission work. This checkpoint changes no Rust source, compiler limit or integration state and runs no compiler engine.

The adopted model preserves the entire previous calibration, including coefficients, point predictions, feature ranges, review thresholds and current-workload targets. It adds an explicit at-most-10% accepted deficit, with provenance pointing to the preserved parent artifact. Previous evidence is unchanged.

Assessment rule

For each measured CPU/RSS median and observed maximum, compare the observation with both its original review threshold and current-workload target:

absolute deficit = observed - original baseline
relative deficit = absolute deficit / original baseline
future optimization target = original baseline
required reduction fraction = absolute deficit / observed

A deficit exists only when observed cost exceeds that baseline. Classify the completed case as follows:

Outcome Meaning
acceptable-for-current-decision Every applicable observation is inside its original baselines.
accepted-with-deficit At least one baseline is exceeded, and every excess is at most 10% of its own original baseline. Record the deficits as future optimization targets; they do not block continued work.
review-needed At least one excess is greater than 10% of its original baseline.
outside-calibration Measurements or prediction inputs are missing/invalid, or the descriptors are outside the retained ranges. This takes precedence over deficit acceptance.

The denominator is always the original exceeded baseline. Allowances do not compound, and adopting an already adopted model cannot enlarge them. Exact rational comparisons of reported decimal numeric values include the 10% boundary without an epsilon. Every known excess remains reportable even if another missing observation or scope failure prevents acceptance.

Each deficit records the measure and units, median/maximum statistic, baseline name and value, observed value, absolute and relative deficit, original optimization target, required absolute reduction and reduction relative to observed cost. A 10% deficit relative to the baseline requires a 9.09% reduction in observed cost to meet it. The point-prediction residual remains a separate diagnostic; the 10% rule applies to review thresholds and targets.

For example, these are synthetic boundary examples, not new measurements:

Baseline Observed Deficit relative to baseline Outcome / future target
CPU review threshold 220 ms 242 ms 22 ms / 10% Accepted with deficit; target 220 ms.
CPU target 300 ms 330 ms 30 ms / 10% Accepted with deficit if every other baseline also fits; target 300 ms.
CPU review threshold 370 ms and target 300 ms 400 ms 8.11% and 33.33% Review required; the acceptable review-threshold deficit cannot hide the larger target deficit.

Future targets are reported data. The tool does not schedule optimization or change compiler stopping policy. Out-of-range results do not become accepted because their physical costs happen to fit a tolerance.

Completed replay and boundary evidence

The primary replay reports 52 acceptable cases, zero accepted deficits, zero review cases and zero outside cases. These existing observations already met the original baselines, so this adoption invents no current optimization deficit.

The structural reference replay retains nine acceptable cases and five outside calibration, with no deficits. Nine reference IDs overlap the primary set; these are separate replays of examined evidence, not 66 independent programs or blind validation. All earlier observations and prediction residuals remain unchanged.

The connected offline self-check exercises fit, calibration, adoption and assessment. The separately indexed synthetic boundaries record 30 evaluator replays: eight acceptable, nine accepted with deficit, ten requiring review and three outside calibration. They cover CPU and RSS, both baseline classes, median-and-maximum and maximum-only excess at zero, exactly 10% and above 10%; multiple baselines; missing observations; and scope/feature failures. Test-only constant point overrides isolate each baseline and never modify the adopted model or recorded measurements.

The exact mathematical RSS target ceiling is 72,089.6 KiB for a 65,536 KiB baseline. The integral-KiB controls accept 72,089 KiB and require review at 72,090 KiB. Fractional-KiB synthetic boundaries test arithmetic and are not presented as GNU time observations. Replaying old models still gives exact JSON-value equality with both prior evaluation artifacts.

Independent charged-work budget review

The read-only work-budget review recommends the following independently measured references for Common's admission work:

Measured primary owner Completed maximum, charged units Rounded nominal budget Separate 10% hard ceiling
Structural Type 1,212,707 1,300,000 1,430,000
Independent Core 1,609,087 1,700,000 1,870,000

These are recommendations, not compiler changes in this checkpoint. The 100,000-unit upward rounding is explicit headroom. The additional 10% margin is relative to the rounded nominal budget, not the observed maximum. Therefore the proposed ceilings are 17.92% and 16.21% above the measured maxima. For a generic integer budget, budget + budget / 10 keeps the margin at most 10% without rounding its fraction upward. No CPU or RSS value is converted into charged units, and cumulative module endpoints are never summed as independent work.

Coverage is limited to the measured primary owners. The builder's separate construction and rewrite owners (dialect.rs) are not recorded by the six phase endpoints; final elaboration emits the elaborator's endpoint before builder completion (elaborate.rs). These measurements cannot alone prove completion under revised ordinary limits.

The retained validator derives its Core limit as three times the shared Type limit (validate.rs). Installing 1,430,000 as that shared hard limit without decoupling would give Core 4,290,000 units, rather than the proposed independent 1,870,000. The validator, ratio-based diagnostic override (calibration.rs) and driver phase labels need consistent treatment. Constraint, usefulness, runtime and unreported builder owners remain separate. Current normative ledger ownership also keeps Core construction and validation separate.

Common owns any production adoption, ordinary-limit executable verification and its serial Cargo window. This worktree provides the review and evidence without building, merging, integrating or changing stopping policy.

Evidence and reproduction

The adoption evidence index authenticates the new model, replays, boundary cases, work-budget review and verification receipt. It also pins previous artifacts without modifying them. The original 52-case inputs and 14-case reference inputs are reused.

From the calibration worktree:

PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 tools/resource_budget_estimates.py self-check
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 tools/resource_budget_estimates.py adopt docs/design/provider-experiments/resource-calibration/decision-budget-model.json --output NEW_ADOPTED_MODEL.json
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 tools/resource_budget_estimates.py evaluate NEW_ADOPTED_MODEL.json docs/design/provider-experiments/resource-calibration/decision-budget-inputs.json
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 tools/resource_budget_estimates.py evaluate NEW_ADOPTED_MODEL.json docs/design/provider-experiments/resource-calibration/decision-budget-reference-inputs.json

To replay the synthetic artifact, load its cases; for each case, copy the adopted model, replace only the declared test-only constant point coefficients, then call evaluate on its input_case. Compare the returned case record with result and expected_status. The artifact retains every test input, override and output; the actual model remains unchanged.

This remains retrospective, host-specific decision support. The previous calibration's program-size, complexity, measurement and validation boundaries continue to apply.
