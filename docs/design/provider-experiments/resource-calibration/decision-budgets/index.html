Provisional decision budgets, 2026-10-08

Development design notes · ash-lang 9a8b73b

Informative design work. Proposals and experiments may be incomplete or superseded. Read the language specification for current rules.

All design notes

This records the user's acceptance of current measured costs for continued feature development under TASK-0035 and DN-0036. The calibrated offline assessment accepts all 52 primary measured cases, including the five realistic SQLite witnesses. No further optimization is required by these measurements. Production compiler stopping limits remain unchanged; this is decision support for the retained experimental baseline.

Predictions, observations and decisions

The original four-term model remains unchanged:

raw prediction = a + b*surface_nodes
                   + c*(type_nodes*type_depth)
                   + d*(resource_sites*surface_nodes)

CPU point prediction = max(raw CPU prediction, 10 ms)
RSS point prediction = raw RSS prediction

CPU review threshold = round_up(2*CPU point prediction + 10 ms, 10 ms)
RSS review threshold = round_up(1.5*RSS point prediction, 4 MiB)

The calibrated artifact retains every original coefficient and charged-unit prediction. The CPU floor rounds the measured zero-width control's 9.199 ms median up to the next 5 ms, giving 10 ms. This complete small-program control is not a universal startup lower bound. The floor prevents implausibly tiny predictions without adding startup to an already fitted whole-process total. Peak RSS already has a fitted fixed cost, so its point prediction needs no additional floor.

These quantities have different roles:

Quantity Role
Point prediction, or budget Rough expected typical cost for the supplied size/complexity descriptors.
Observation Measured CPU, process peak RSS or separately charged units; each retains its own units.
Review threshold A deliberately loose tolerance for deciding whether to investigate a prediction miss. It is not a prediction or statistical confidence bound.
Current-workload target A chosen engineering objective of 300 ms CPU and 64 MiB peak RSS for the current workload set on this host.
Compiler stopping limit A separate admission policy; this batch changes none of its constants or behavior.

The threshold multipliers, rounding and engineering targets are pragmatic choices made after examining the evidence. They are not estimates of a universal maximum. Formula thresholds and targets remain separate: persistence has a 370 ms CPU review threshold and a 300 ms engineering target. Neither is silently substituted for the other.

An assessment reports acceptable-for-current-decision only when completed CPU and RSS observations have at least three measurements, both medians and observed maxima meet their review thresholds and targets, and descriptors lie inside the original model's feature ranges. Prediction alone cannot accept an unmeasured program. New combinations remain unvalidated even when each individual descriptor lies inside those ranges.

The largest measured CPU value is 259.204 ms and the largest peak RSS is 42.5625 MiB, both from persistence. Copying still costs approximately 1.75 times its point CPU prediction, and its Source charge estimate still misses 1,212,707 charged units against a prediction near 476,786. Those misses are visible; the decision accepts their modest physical cost rather than claiming a more accurate model or demanding an optimization.

Charged-unit predictions remain diagnostic. Charges include reservations and are not executed instruction counts. Their ratios alone do not determine whether optimization is required. CPU and RSS are measured separately and are not converted into charged units.

The original whole-pipeline Ash measurements used the finite experimental diagnostic allowance, with the ordinary limits unchanged. Acceptable physical cost does not establish admission under ordinary production limits. Any production admission-policy change remains separate work; these results do not require optimization as its prerequisite.

Coverage and retained uncertainty

The primary replay assesses all 44 original CPU-precision cases and eight previously examined language fixtures. All 52 meet both the physical review thresholds and targets, including their observed maxima. This is explicit recalibration on examined data, not blind validation. The fitted coefficients were not refitted, and repetitions are not additional independent programs.

The CPU floor reduces RMSE on the original 44 cases from 26.30 to 26.09 ms, and on the eight basic fixtures from 6.40 to 2.96 ms. The combined 52-case CPU RMSE is 24.03 ms; RSS RMSE remains 2.73 MiB. These absolute errors describe this evidence, not predicted accuracy for arbitrary programs. The model still overpredicts several structural examples and underpredicts copying.

The 14-case structural cross-check also meets all physical thresholds and targets. Nine IDs overlap the primary set; the cross-check is kept separate rather than counted as additional independent programs. Nine reference cases are inside the original feature ranges; five retain outside-calibration status: the zero-width startup control and type/prerequisite depths 24 and 32. Their measured costs are acceptable, but their features do not become validated interpolation merely because their costs fit a loose tolerance. No feature range was expanded to hide these flags.

These targets concern the current workload set and host: Linux x86-64, AMD Ryzen 7 7435HS, optimized Ash builds, fixed native dependencies and the recorded measurement boundary. Larger programs, different type/effect/resource compositions, another host or another compiler version need new measurements and an explicit reassessment. Target wall time remains observational because scheduling and I/O can add delays independently of CPU cost.

How to use the result

Continue feature development on this baseline. Compare a completed program's actual CPU and peak RSS with its point predictions, review thresholds and stated workload targets. Keep prediction residuals visible. An assessment outside tolerance requests review of measurement noise, workload changes, model scope and physical cost; it does not automatically require optimization. Consider optimization when repeated measurements establish a material practical problem, rather than from a charged-unit miss alone.

A missing measurement or an out-of-range descriptor reports outside-calibration. Choose a new workload target explicitly when the program size or complexity changes; the current 300 ms / 64 MiB targets do not become universal compiler limits. The tool does not run the compiler, change allowances, refuse programs or schedule optimization.

Evidence and reproduction

The evidence index authenticates the derived input snapshots, model, replay, tool and verification receipt. The 52-case input snapshot, startup control and structural reference snapshot preserve their source-summary hashes. The original measurements, old model and previous evidence indexes remain unchanged.

The connected offline self-check exercises fit, calibration, prediction and measured assessment together, including a maximum-only overrun, a missing observation and an outside-range case. Stored-data replay supplies the real integration check. This batch ran no Ash, Rust, Cargo or C compiler process and required no compiler window.

From this worktree, reproduce with the preserved inputs:

PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 tools/resource_budget_estimates.py self-check
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 tools/resource_budget_estimates.py calibrate docs/design/provider-experiments/resource-calibration/rough-budget-model.json docs/design/provider-experiments/resource-calibration/decision-budget-startup.json --output NEW_MODEL.json
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 tools/resource_budget_estimates.py evaluate NEW_MODEL.json docs/design/provider-experiments/resource-calibration/decision-budget-inputs.json
