Ash benefits from careful questions as much as implementation work. Begin with the current behavior, make the uncertainty concrete, and keep claims proportional to the evidence.

## Explore the language

Run the [tour](/learn/tour/), try a small variation, and compare the result with the [specification]({{source.ash-lang}}/blob/{{checkpoint.revision}}/spec/README.md). A minimal program and its exact command, compiler revision, output, and expected behavior make a useful report.

Use the [Ash repository]({{source.ash-lang}}) to find current contribution and issue entry points. Read its contribution guidance before changing language or compiler code.

## Research a language question

The [design index]({{source.ash-lang}}/blob/{{checkpoint.revision}}/docs/design/README.md) and [development trajectory]({{source.ash-lang}}/blob/{{checkpoint.revision}}/docs/trajectory.md) show current investigations and dependencies. Distinguish a research idea from accepted semantics. A prototype or passing example can supply evidence without establishing a new language guarantee.

## Improve learning material

Study lessons live in [ash-doc]({{source.ash-doc}}). Its contribution guidance governs prose, executable examples, evidence, and edition targets. Keep a lesson's explanation, canonical program, and checks aligned.

Website introductions and navigation belong to ash-site. We welcome clearer explanations, broken-link reports, and examples that help readers evaluate the language. Do not silently copy a study chapter into website content.

## Choose a bounded change

Before starting, identify the reader or programmer problem, the source of truth, and what would demonstrate an improvement. Follow the current upstream workflow rather than a historical article's instructions.

If you are just looking around, [language status](/status/) and [Study Ash](/study/) are good next stops.
